Calm isn't magic. It's neuroscience.

Your brain runs on electrical rhythms. Slow, steady practices like breathing and meditation are linked to calmer rhythms and a calmer body. Tap a brain wave to see it move.

💬

Name it to tame it

Neuroimaging studies suggest that putting feelings into words, called affect labeling, is linked to a quieter amygdala, the brain's alarm centre. That is why daily mood check-ins matter.

🌬️

Breathwork

Slow breathing, around six breaths a minute, is associated with higher heart-rate variability, a marker of your body's calming response. Long exhales are the simplest reset you own.

🧘

Meditation

Across many studies, regular mindfulness practice is associated with lower perceived stress and better attention. Even short daily sessions count.

🤸

Yoga and movement

Yoga joins gentle movement, breath and attention. Research links regular practice with lower stress and better mood, and daily movement supports sleep.
